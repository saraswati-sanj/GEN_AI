# PubMed Peer-Reviewed Research Summaries on Food Additives & Health

## 1. Dietary Emulsifiers and Gut Microbiota Disruption
- **Study Summary (Nature 2015, Gastroenterology 2021)**:
  - Common industrial food emulsifiers such as **Carboxymethylcellulose (CMC / INS 466)**, **Polysorbate 80 (INS 433)**, and **Mono- and Diglycerides of Fatty Acids (INS 471)** alter the human gut microbiome composition.
  - Findings indicate emulsifiers erode the protective intestinal mucus layer, promoting low-grade intestinal inflammation, systemic bacterial translocation, metabolic syndrome, and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) exacerbation.

## 2. Artificial Sweeteners, Glycemic Control, and Cardiometabolic Risk
- **Study Summary (BMJ 2022, Cell 2022)**:
  - Evaluation of artificial sweeteners (**Sucralose INS 955, Saccharin INS 954, Aspartame INS 951, Acesulfame Potassium INS 950**).
  - Randomized controlled trials demonstrate that non-nutritive sweeteners induce personalized glycemic responses by altering gut microbiome function.
  - Large cohort study (NutriNet-Santé) showed a direct association between higher consumption of artificial sweeteners (particularly aspartame and acesulfame-K) and increased cardiovascular disease risk (hazard ratio 1.09).

## 3. Synthetic Food Dyes and Neurodevelopmental Effects
- **Study Summary (Lancet 2007, California OEHHA Report 2021)**:
  - Double-blind placebo-controlled trials on synthetic food colors (**Tartrazine INS 102, Sunset Yellow INS 110, Carmoisine INS 122, Allura Red INS 129**).
  - Consumption of artificial food color mixtures leads to increased hyperactivity and attention deficit symptoms in general pediatric populations.

## 4. Nitrites, Nitrates, and Colorectal Carcinogenesis
- **Study Summary (IARC Monograph 93, Lancet Oncology 2015)**:
  - **Sodium Nitrite (INS 250)** and **Sodium Nitrate (INS 251)** used as preservatives in cured meats.
  - React with dietary amines under acidic stomach conditions to form N-nitroso compounds (NOCs), classified by WHO IARC as Group 1 (Carcinogenic to humans) for colorectal cancer risk.

## 5. Ultra-Processed Food (UPF) Consumption and All-Cause Mortality
- **Study Summary (BMJ 2019, JAMA Internal Medicine 2019)**:
  - Multi-cohort prospective study following over 100,000 adults over 10 years.
  - Each 10% increase in the proportion of ultra-processed foods in the diet was associated with a 12% increase in risk of overall cardiovascular disease and a 14% increase in all-cause mortality.
