# WHO (World Health Organization) Global Nutritional Guidelines & Standards

## 1. Free Sugars Recommendation
- WHO strongly recommends reducing free sugar intake to less than 10% of total daily energy intake in both adults and children.
- A further reduction to **below 5% of total energy intake** (approx. 25 grams or 6 teaspoons per day for a standard 2,000 kcal diet) provides conditional health benefits, significantly reducing dental caries, obesity, and type 2 diabetes risk.
- **Definition of Free Sugars**: All monosaccharides and disaccharides added to foods and drinks by the manufacturer, cook, or consumer, plus sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices, and fruit juice concentrates.

## 2. Sodium and Salt Intake Standards
- WHO recommends adults consume **less than 2,000 mg of sodium per day**, equivalent to less than **5 grams of salt (approx. 1 teaspoon)**.
- High sodium consumption (>2g/day) combined with insufficient potassium intake (<3.5g/day) contributes to high blood pressure, elevated cardiovascular disease risk, stroke, and chronic kidney damage.
- Ultra-processed convenience products (instant noodles, chips, sauces, processed meats) account for up to 75% of total dietary sodium intake in modernized populations.

## 3. Fats and Fatty Acids Guidelines
- **Total Fat Intake**: Should not exceed **30% of total energy intake** to prevent unhealthy weight gain and non-communicable diseases (NCDs).
- **Saturated Fatty Acids (SFA)**: Should be restricted to less than **10% of total energy intake**. Replace SFAs with polyunsaturated fats (PUFAs) and monounsaturated fats (MUFAs).
- **Trans-Fatty Acids (TFA)**: Total trans fats must be limited to less than **1% of total energy intake** (approx. 2.2g/day for a 2,000 kcal diet). WHO advocates for global elimination of industrially-produced trans fatty acids (REPLACE initiative).

## 4. Ultra-Processed Foods (UPF) and Non-Sugar Sweeteners (NSS)
- **NSS Advisory (2023 Guidelines)**: WHO advises against using Non-Sugar Sweeteners (such as Aspartame, Sucralose, Saccharin, Stevia, Acesulfame K) for weight control or reducing the risk of NCDs. Long-term observational studies suggest potential increased risk of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and mortality with habitual NSS consumption.
- **Ultra-Processed Foods Impact**: Diets high in ultra-processed products are systematically associated with higher risks of cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, depression, gastrointestinal disorders, and all-cause mortality.
