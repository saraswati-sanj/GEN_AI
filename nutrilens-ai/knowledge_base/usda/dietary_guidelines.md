# USDA Dietary Guidelines for Americans (2020–2025)

## 1. Daily Reference Intakes (DRI) & Tolerable Upper Intake Levels (UL)
- **Added Sugars**: Less than 10% of total daily calories starting at age 2 (approx. 50g for a 2,000 calorie diet). Children under 2 years should avoid added sugars entirely.
- **Saturated Fat**: Less than 10% of total daily calories starting at age 2.
- **Sodium**:
  - Adults and adolescents (14+ years): <2,300 mg/day (equivalent to ~1 teaspoon of table sugar/salt).
  - Children 9-13 years: <1,800 mg/day.
  - Children 4-8 years: <1,500 mg/day.
- **Dietary Fiber**:
  - Adult Women (19-50 years): 25-28 g/day.
  - Adult Men (19-50 years): 31-34 g/day.

## 2. Nutrient Density and Processing Risk Framework
- Nutrient-dense foods supply vitamins, minerals, and other beneficial substances with little or no added sugars, saturated fats, or sodium.
- **Nutrient Dilution**: Commercial food products with refined starches, high fructose corn syrup (HFCS), and hydrogenated oils dilute nutrient density while providing empty calories.
- **Trans Fats (PHO)**: USDA alignment with FDA FDA GRAS revocation of Partially Hydrogenated Oils. Trans fat intake increases LDL cholesterol while lowering HDL cholesterol, escalating coronary heart disease risk.

## 3. High Risk Ingredient Indicators
- **High Fructose Corn Syrup (HFCS-55)**: Metabolized primarily in the liver, stimulating de novo lipogenesis, fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and insulin resistance.
- **Palm Oil & Palm Olein**: High in saturated palmitic acid (C16:0). Elevates plasma total cholesterol and atherogenic LDL cholesterol.
- **Refined Wheat Flour (Enriched Bleached Flour)**: High GI carbohydrate stripped of bran and germ, causing rapid glycemic elevation.
